HISTORY
The Underground Railroad: Freedom's Secret Network
The Underground Railroad was not a literal railroad but a vast, clandestine network of safe houses, secret routes, and brave conductors that helped enslaved African Americans escape to freedom between the 1780s and 1860s. Harriet Tubman — herself an escaped enslaved woman — made 13 dangerous missions back into the South, guiding approximately 70 people to freedom. She never lost a single passenger, earning her the name "Moses of her People."
WHY IT MATTERS
The Underground Railroad stands as one of history's greatest acts of collective resistance — proof that ordinary people, acting with extraordinary courage, can dismantle systems of oppression.
